The pole at the end of a street always looks a little different from the ones in the middle. A steel wire runs from near the top down to the ground at an angle, with a yellow plastic guard over the bottom section so nobody walks into it. If it were not there, the pole would slowly lean towards the cables and one day it would fail.

That wire is a down guy, and the steel rod it is tied to is an anchor. Together they balance the pull of the cables.

## Why tension has to be balanced

A messenger strand or self-supporting cable is tensioned between poles so that it does not sag too far. On a straight line of poles that tension pulls equally in both directions and the pole in the middle feels almost no net sideways force. At a corner the two pulls no longer cancel, and the pole is dragged towards the inside of the bend. At a dead-end the full tension pulls one way with nothing to balance it.

A guy takes that unbalanced load and carries it to the ground. The pole then works mostly in compression, which is what a pole is good at, instead of bending, which is what breaks it.

## Types of guy

A down guy runs straight from the pole to an anchor in the ground. A sidewalk guy adds a horizontal strut so that the wire clears a footpath before angling down to the anchor. A span guy runs horizontally to a stub pole or another pole, which then has its own down guy.

Guy strand is galvanized steel wire rope, fixed to the pole with a thimble-eye bolt and to the anchor rod with a preformed guy grip or a three-bolt clamp. Where a guy passes through the supply space, strain insulators are inserted so it cannot carry voltage to the ground.

## Anchors and the ground they sit in

| Anchor type | Where it suits | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Screw or helix anchor | Most soils | Driven in by machine, quick to install |
| Expanding anchor | Firm soils | Set in a drilled hole, expands when tightened |
| Plate anchor | Soft or sandy soils | Large bearing area, dug in and backfilled |
| Rock anchor | Rock | Grouted or wedged into a drilled hole |

Holding capacity depends on soil far more than on the anchor itself. The other key number is the lead, the horizontal distance from the pole base to the anchor. A lead roughly equal to the attachment height gives a comfortable angle. A short lead makes the angle steep, which raises the tension in the guy and the downward force on the pole.

## Terminology in other countries

In India and Australia the same hardware is usually called a stay rather than a guy. You will hear stay wire, stay rod, stay plate and turnbuckle. The engineering is identical; only the words change.

## Final thoughts

Guys are easy to overlook. They are also the first thing a pole owner checks when a new cable adds tension at a corner. If your design adds load at a dead-end or a bend, assume it needs a guy until the calculation proves otherwise.
