The day before a drill rig arrives, a different truck shows up, with a big tank on the back, a long hose, and a lot of noise. A worker points a wand at the grass where a yellow paint line crosses the planned bore, and within minutes there is a clean, square hole with a plastic gas pipe at the bottom, undamaged. Someone measures the depth, writes it down, takes a photo, and fills the hole in.

That is potholing, and it is the cheapest insurance a construction crew can buy.

## Why paint marks are not enough

Utility locating services mark the ground with paint and flags. In the United States the color code comes from the American Public Works Association (APWA): red for electric, yellow for gas, orange for communications, blue for potable water, green for sewer, and white for proposed excavation. Those marks are useful, but they come with a tolerance zone. The utility is somewhere within a band either side of the line, and the mark says nothing reliable about depth.

A drill head or plow blade does not work in bands. It is at one exact point and depth. The only way to know that point is clear is to see the utility.

## How vacuum excavation works

Vacuum excavation breaks up the soil with pressurized water (hydro excavation) or compressed air (air excavation) and sucks the loosened material into a tank on the truck. Neither uses a hard edge, so a plastic gas service or buried power cable is not cut the way a shovel or backhoe tooth would cut it. Air excavation leaves dry spoil that can go straight back in the hole; hydro is faster in hard or clay soils but produces slurry to dispose of.

## What a good pothole record contains

The point of potholing is the record, not the hole. In subsurface utility engineering, described in the ASCE 38 standard from the American Society of Civil Engineers, exposing a utility gives the highest data quality level.

| Quality level | How the data was obtained | What you can trust |
| --- | --- | --- |
| D | Existing records only | Approximate presence |
| C | Records plus surface features such as valves and manholes | Rough horizontal position |
| B | Surface geophysical survey, such as electromagnetic locating | Horizontal position |
| A | Physically exposed by potholing and surveyed | Horizontal and vertical position, size, material |

For each pothole I expect at least the utility type and material, its diameter, the depth to the top of the pipe, the horizontal offset from a fixed reference such as the curb, and a photo with a tape in it. That record goes into the bore plan and later the as-built.

## Practice by country

In the United States, calling 811 before digging is required by law in every state, and potholing within the tolerance zone is standard on drilled routes. Australia has Before You Dig Australia, which supplies asset owners' plans rather than field marks, so potholing carries even more weight. In India there is no single locate service and records are often incomplete, so hand-dug trial pits are the normal way to confirm services, and the design should budget for them.

## Final thoughts

Every utility strike I have heard about had the same story: someone was sure they knew where it was. A pothole replaces being sure with knowing. How does your team decide which crossings get potholed and which get drilled on trust?
